# Chapter 11: Test Questions - Identifying and Managing AI Risks

> Practice questions in the style of the AIGP certification exam (single-best-answer multiple choice, 4 options). Try to answer all questions before checking the answer key at the bottom.

## Questions

1. What is the main purpose of an "AI risk taxonomy"?
   A. To eliminate the need for risk assessments
   B. To provide a structured classification of risk types to support consistent identification and management
   C. To classify programming languages
   D. To certify AI vendors

2. What is "AI red teaming"?
   A. A marketing exercise to promote AI products
   B. An adversarial testing process in which testers attempt to find vulnerabilities, harmful outputs, or failure modes in an AI system
   C. A method of training data labeling
   D. A financial audit technique

3. What is a "jailbreak" in the context of LLM security?
   A. A hardware failure mode
   B. A technique used to bypass a model's safety guardrails to elicit prohibited or harmful outputs
   C. A method for compressing model weights
   D. A legal process for patent protection

4. Which of the following best describes a "runtime guardrail"?
   A. A control applied only during model training
   B. A control (e.g., content filter, input/output validation) applied during live operation to constrain AI system behavior
   C. A legal contract term
   D. A hardware cooling mechanism

5. Why might an organization consider AI-specific insurance coverage?
   A. Because all general liability policies automatically cover AI incidents
   B. To address potential financial exposure from AI-related incidents not clearly covered by traditional policies
   C. Because it eliminates the need for any other risk controls
   D. Because it is legally required everywhere

6. What distinguishes a "threat model" from a general risk assessment in AI security practice?
   A. They are identical processes
   B. A threat model specifically analyzes potential attackers, attack vectors, and system vulnerabilities, often using structured frameworks
   C. Threat models apply only to physical security
   D. Threat models eliminate the need for red teaming

7. Which of the following is an example of a "data poisoning" attack?
   A. Encrypting a model's weights for secure storage
   B. Deliberately introducing corrupted or malicious data into a training set to manipulate model behavior
   C. Updating a model's documentation
   D. Running a standard unit test

8. What risk domain does "model inversion" primarily relate to?
   A. Financial reporting risk
   B. Privacy risk - potentially reconstructing sensitive training data from model outputs
   C. Physical safety risk
   D. Intellectual property licensing risk

9. Why is scoping (defining what systems/layers are in and out of bounds) important before conducting red teaming?
   A. Scoping is unnecessary and slows testing
   B. It ensures testers and defenders understand boundaries, reducing the risk of unintended harm during testing
   C. It guarantees no vulnerabilities will be found
   D. It is only relevant for physical penetration testing

10. Which of the following best reflects the purpose of "defense-in-depth" in AI security risk management?
    A. Relying on a single control to address all risks
    B. Layering multiple, complementary safeguards so that if one control fails, others still mitigate the risk
    C. Avoiding any technical controls in favor of policy alone
    D. Applying controls only after an incident occurs

## Answers

1. B
2. B
3. B
4. B
5. B
6. B
7. B
8. B
9. B
10. B
