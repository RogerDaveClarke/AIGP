## Summary
An AI Governance program is critical to the successful and safe use of AI systems by both employees and customers. This summary will decompose the chapter into the what, the why, the where, the who and framework best practices

**The What:** An AI Governance Program is an organization wide system of people, process and controls that apply to the development, deployment and oversight of AI systems in a responsible manner that prioritizes organization values and complies with applicable laws and regulations. 

**The Why:** An organization that has a well formed and functioning program can enjot benefits such as, for example, lower risk, fostering innovation, earning trust with customers, improving quality and ensuring regulatory compliance. Collectively, the program when implemented and enforced, gives an organization a competitive advantage.

**The Where:** A Governance Program is by its very nature cross-functional requiring expertise, input and decisions from many stakeholders. In order for it to be successful, committed to, adopted, persist and adapt, it must have executive sponsorship. The officers best placed for this role are: Chief Privacy, Chief Information, Chief Legal, Chief Information Security Officer. The inherent risk is that each may prioritize their own domain, undervaluing the others leading to a biased implementation of the progress and how its run. An effective structure must account for these tendencies by assigning clear ownership to the most appropriate function and requie close collaboration with the others.

**The Who**: This is the group of people and entities that influence, contribute to, or are affected by the organization's strategy including internal and external stakeholders. This part of the program should create an ecosystem map that establishes clear lines of communications using the following steps: Identfy stakeholders, Categorize and Prioritize stakeholders, Develop and implement engagement policies and Monitor and adjust.

## Framework Best Practices 
 - **Executive Sponsorship:** This is the cornerstone of the program setting the tone and signaling that responsible AI is a strategic priority, provides resources, clear pathway for decision making, ensures the board has visibility, ensures RAI is everyone's responsibility and ensures the organization's values and norms are embedded.
 - **Risk Assessments:** These provide the clarity to guide the decision make apparatus and are based on ISO/IEC 42005 starting with mapping the current and planned use of AI.
 - **Policies, Processes and Controls:** Policies set clear expectations, processes translate those into actions and repeatable workflows and controls ensure that the processes are followed and remain effective over time. Common policies are: AI Deployment, Risk and Data Management, Monitoring, Intake and Acceptable Use
 - **Training and Awareness:** Implementing such a program is an exercise in change management requiring the audience to clearly understand: Why. How the changes affect their work, their roles, responsibilities, expectations of them and the benefits. The organization needs to create a training program with mandatory and monitored completion, additional accessible paths to learning and drive awareness campaigns.   
 - **Issue Report and Feedback Channels:** What distinguishes a resilient program from a fragile one is not the absence of problems but a clear pathway to raise them. These channels provide a early warning system enabling the organisation to address a risk before it escalates into a costly monster. Channels need to be easy to find and use, are frequently monitored, submission does not result in retaliation, and the loop is visibly closed on all issues. The organization should provide metrics including TTM and these should be available and transparent to all.
