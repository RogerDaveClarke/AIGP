# Chapter 6: Test Questions - How Existing Laws Intersect with AI

> Practice questions in the style of the AIGP certification exam (single-best-answer multiple choice, 4 options). Try to answer all questions before checking the answer key at the bottom.

## Questions

1. Under current U.S. Copyright Office guidance, what is generally required for AI-assisted output to be eligible for copyright protection?
   A. The AI system must be registered as the author
   B. Sufficient human authorship/creative control must be present in the work
   C. No human involvement is required at all
   D. Copyright never applies to any AI-assisted work

2. Which GDPR concept most directly governs the lawful basis for processing personal data used to train an AI model?
   A. Article 6 (lawfulness of processing)
   B. Article 17 only (right to erasure)
   C. Article 82 only (right to compensation)
   D. Recital 1 only

3. Which U.S. federal law is most commonly cited as a basis for employment-related anti-discrimination claims involving AI-powered hiring tools?
   A. Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964
   B. The GDPR
   C. The EU AI Act
   D. HIPAA

4. Under general products liability principles, what is often cited as a unique challenge in applying "defect" analysis to AI systems?
   A. AI systems never change after deployment
   B. AI systems can evolve with use/context, so a defect may not be knowable or fixed at the time of market placement
   C. AI systems are not considered "products" anywhere
   D. There is no applicable liability theory for software

5. Which legal theory is most relevant to allegations of AI-enabled algorithmic price-fixing among competitors?
   A. Antitrust/competition law
   B. Copyright law
   C. Patent law
   D. Products liability law

6. Under current USPTO guidance, can a fully AI-generated invention (with no human inventor contribution) receive a U.S. patent?
   A. Yes, AI systems can be listed as sole inventors
   B. No, inventorship generally requires a natural person's significant contribution
   C. Only if the AI is open source
   D. Only for pharmaceutical inventions

7. Which consumer protection authority in the U.S. has brought enforcement actions against companies for deceptive AI-related claims?
   A. Federal Trade Commission (FTC)
   B. U.S. Copyright Office
   C. USPTO
   D. OECD

8. What is the general relationship between existing (non-AI-specific) laws and AI systems, as framed in this chapter?
   A. Existing laws automatically become void when AI is involved
   B. Many existing laws (privacy, IP, consumer protection, anti-discrimination, liability, competition) already apply to AI, even without AI-specific statutes
   C. AI systems are entirely outside the scope of any existing law
   D. Only criminal law applies to AI

9. Which of the following best describes "fair use" as a potential defense in AI copyright training-data disputes in the U.S.?
   A. It automatically permits any use of copyrighted material for AI training
   B. It is a fact-specific defense that may excuse otherwise infringing uses depending on factors like purpose and market effect
   C. It only applies outside the United States
   D. It eliminates the need for any copyright analysis

10. Why are anti-discrimination laws (e.g., disparate impact theories) particularly relevant to AI governance?
    A. Because AI systems cannot process demographic-adjacent data
    B. Because AI systems used in decisions like hiring or lending can produce disparate outcomes across protected groups, even without intentional bias
    C. Because anti-discrimination laws exempt automated decision-making
    D. Because this issue only arises in the EU

## Answers

1. B
2. A
3. A
4. B
5. A
6. B
7. A
8. B
9. B
10. B
