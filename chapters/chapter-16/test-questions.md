# Chapter 16: Test Questions - AI Agents

> Practice questions in the style of the AIGP certification exam (single-best-answer multiple choice, 4 options). Try to answer all questions before checking the answer key at the bottom.

## Questions

1. What best defines an "AI agent" as distinguished from a standard generative AI chatbot?
   A. An agent only generates static text responses with no further action
   B. An agent can autonomously plan and execute multi-step actions/tools to achieve a goal, often with limited human intervention
   C. Agents cannot use external tools
   D. Agents are identical to traditional rule-based scripts with no AI component

2. What role does the "Model Context Protocol (MCP)" play in the AI agent ecosystem?
   A. It is a legal framework for agent liability
   B. It is a technical standard for connecting AI models/agents to external tools and data sources in a consistent way
   C. It is a government regulation specific to agents
   D. It replaces the need for agent testing

3. Which of the following is a distinctive risk introduced by autonomous AI agents compared to single-turn generative AI systems?
   A. Agents cannot cause any real-world effects
   B. Agents may take a chain of unintended or harmful actions across multiple steps/systems with reduced human oversight
   C. Agents are inherently safer because they use less compute
   D. Agents eliminate the need for any guardrails

4. What is "authenticated delegation" in the context of AI agents?
   A. A method for compressing agent model weights
   B. A mechanism for verifying that an agent is authorized to act on behalf of a specific human or organization for given tasks
   C. A type of encryption algorithm unrelated to agents
   D. A legal term with no technical implementation

5. How might existing law (e.g., agency law, contract law) apply to AI agents performing actions on behalf of users or organizations?
   A. Existing legal doctrines are entirely inapplicable to AI agents
   B. Courts and regulators may look to analogous legal concepts, such as agency and liability principles, while new AI-specific rules are still developing
   C. AI agents are automatically granted independent legal personhood everywhere
   D. No law anywhere currently contemplates agent actions

6. What governance control is commonly recommended to limit the "blast radius" of an AI agent's actions?
   A. Granting agents unrestricted access to all systems
   B. Scoping agent permissions/tool access narrowly and requiring human approval for high-impact actions
   C. Removing all logging of agent activity
   D. Disabling any kill-switch mechanisms

7. What does a "multi-agent system" refer to?
   A. A single monolithic model with no subcomponents
   B. An architecture where multiple AI agents coordinate or interact (e.g., via orchestration) to complete complex tasks
   C. A deprecated AI architecture no longer in use
   D. A system exclusively used for image generation

8. Why are benchmarks like GAIA, OSWorld, and WebArena relevant to AI agent governance?
   A. They have no relevance to evaluating agent capability or risk
   B. They provide standardized ways to evaluate agent performance and reliability on realistic, complex tasks
   C. They are legal certification requirements
   D. They apply only to non-agentic chatbots

9. What is a key reason organizations are advised to log and monitor AI agent actions in detail?
   A. Logging has no governance value for autonomous systems
   B. Detailed logs support incident investigation, accountability, and detection of unintended or harmful agent behavior
   C. Logging is prohibited for agentic systems
   D. Agents cannot be monitored by design

10. Which best reflects the current state of AI-agent-specific law compared to general AI law?
    A. AI-agent-specific legislation is already comprehensive and settled in every major jurisdiction
    B. Law specific to autonomous AI agents is still emerging, with much reliance on existing general AI and sectoral legal frameworks in the interim
    C. Agents are entirely unregulated and will remain so
    D. Agent law has existed since before general AI law

## Answers

1. B
2. B
3. B
4. B
5. B
6. B
7. B
8. B
9. B
10. B
