# Chapter 3: Test Questions - Key Stakeholders in AI Governance

> Practice questions in the style of the AIGP certification exam (single-best-answer multiple choice, 4 options). Try to answer all questions before checking the answer key at the bottom.

## Questions

1. Which of the following best describes the role of an "AI Governance Professional" as it is emerging in the field?
   A. A role focused solely on writing AI model code
   B. A cross-disciplinary role combining legal, technical, ethical, and risk management expertise to oversee responsible AI use
   C. A purely marketing-focused role
   D. A role limited to data entry for AI training sets

2. In the AI governance ecosystem, which stakeholder group is primarily responsible for setting generally applicable legal requirements for AI systems?
   A. Standards development organizations
   B. Regulators and legislators
   C. AI vendors
   D. Internal audit teams

3. What is the role of standards development organizations (e.g., ISO, NIST) in the AI governance ecosystem?
   A. Enforcing criminal penalties for AI misuse
   B. Developing voluntary technical and management standards that organizations can adopt to operationalize governance
   C. Drafting binding international treaties
   D. Certifying individual AI practitioners only

4. Which skill area is commonly identified as essential for AI governance professionals, beyond technical AI literacy?
   A. Advanced hardware engineering
   B. Legal and regulatory literacy, risk management, and ethics
   C. Exclusive expertise in marketing analytics
   D. Proficiency in unrelated, non-AI software testing only

5. Within an organization, why might AI governance responsibilities be distributed across multiple roles (e.g., legal, privacy, security, data science) rather than centralized in one role?
   A. Because AI governance has no cross-functional impact
   B. Because AI risks intersect with many existing domains of expertise, requiring coordinated distributed ownership
   C. Because centralization is prohibited by law
   D. Because AI governance professionals do not exist

6. Which of the following external stakeholders would typically be included when mapping the AI governance ecosystem?
   A. Civil society organizations and advocacy groups
   B. Only internal engineering teams
   C. Only the organization's shareholders
   D. Only competitors

7. What is a key reason AI literacy training is emphasized for non-technical employees under emerging AI governance/legal requirements (e.g., EU AI Act Article 4)?
   A. To ensure all employees can personally fine-tune models
   B. To help staff understand the capabilities, limitations, and risks of AI systems they interact with
   C. To replace legal compliance training entirely
   D. It is not a recognized governance practice

8. Which best describes the purpose of an "AI governance skills framework"?
   A. A rubric for hiring only data scientists
   B. A structured way to identify the competencies (legal, technical, ethical, risk) needed across roles involved in AI governance
   C. A certification mandated by law in all countries
   D. A framework exclusively for evaluating AI model accuracy

9. Professional certifications like AIGP primarily aim to:
   A. Replace the need for legal counsel in all AI matters
   B. Validate a practitioner's knowledge of AI governance principles, laws, and practices
   C. Certify specific AI models as safe
   D. Grant a government license to build AI systems

10. Why is it important to map "who is an AI governance professional" across an organization rather than assume a single title covers the role?
    A. Because title standardization has already fully occurred industry-wide
    B. Because the function is still emerging and is often performed by people with varied titles and backgrounds
    C. Because only lawyers can perform AI governance tasks
    D. Because it has no bearing on accountability

## Answers

1. B
2. B
3. B
4. B
5. B
6. A
7. B
8. B
9. B
10. B
