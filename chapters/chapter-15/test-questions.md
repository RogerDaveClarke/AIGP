# Chapter 15: Test Questions - The AI Cyber Threat Landscape

> Practice questions in the style of the AIGP certification exam (single-best-answer multiple choice, 4 options). Try to answer all questions before checking the answer key at the bottom.

## Questions

1. Which of the following best describes "prompt injection" as an AI-specific threat type?
   A. A hardware failure in GPU clusters
   B. Manipulating an AI system's input (directly or indirectly) to cause it to ignore instructions or perform unintended actions
   C. A method of encrypting model weights
   D. A legal claim related to copyright

2. What distinguishes "indirect" prompt injection from "direct" prompt injection?
   A. There is no meaningful distinction
   B. Indirect injection embeds malicious instructions in external content (e.g., a webpage or document) later processed by the AI system, rather than being typed directly by the user
   C. Indirect injection only affects image models
   D. Indirect injection requires physical access to servers

3. What is the primary concern behind "data poisoning" attacks on AI training pipelines?
   A. They have no effect on model output
   B. Attackers can corrupt training data to manipulate model behavior or introduce backdoors
   C. They only affect model documentation
   D. They are exclusively a privacy concern, not a security one

4. Which organization's framework (ATLAS) is specifically designed to catalog adversarial tactics and techniques against AI systems, similar to ATT&CK for traditional cybersecurity?
   A. OECD
   B. MITRE
   C. ISO
   D. FTC

5. What is a "deepfake" as discussed in the AI cyber threat landscape?
   A. A traditional phishing email
   B. Synthetic media (e.g., video, audio, images) generated or manipulated by AI to convincingly depict events or statements that did not occur
   C. A type of firewall configuration
   D. A legitimate business analytics report

6. How can AI itself be used defensively in cybersecurity, per the chapter's discussion?
   A. AI has no defensive security applications
   B. AI can help detect anomalies, triage threats, and automate aspects of threat hunting and response
   C. AI can only be used offensively by attackers
   D. AI defensive use is illegal in most jurisdictions

7. What does "model inversion" risk allow a malicious actor to potentially achieve?
   A. Permanently disable a model
   B. Infer or reconstruct sensitive information from training data based on model outputs
   C. Automatically patch security vulnerabilities
   D. Bypass copyright law entirely

8. Why are nation-state and hacktivist threat actors specifically discussed as relevant to AI systems?
   A. They have no interest in AI technology
   B. They may target or exploit AI systems for espionage, disruption, or influence operations, among other objectives
   C. They are legally barred from using AI
   D. They only target non-AI systems

9. What is the purpose of adopting a "defense-in-depth" strategy against AI-specific threats like jailbreaks and prompt injection?
   A. To rely entirely on a single guardrail
   B. To layer multiple complementary controls (e.g., input filtering, output validation, monitoring) since no single control is fully reliable
   C. To eliminate the need for any human review
   D. To avoid any investment in AI security

10. Why does the chapter describe AI as causing a "sea change" in the cybersecurity threat landscape?
    A. AI has had no meaningful impact on cybersecurity
    B. AI introduces novel attack surfaces and also significantly changes the scale, sophistication, and speed of both attacks and defenses
    C. AI only affects physical security, not cybersecurity
    D. Cybersecurity practices remain entirely unchanged by AI

## Answers

1. B
2. B
3. B
4. B
5. B
6. B
7. B
8. B
9. B
10. B
