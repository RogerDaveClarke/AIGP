# Chapter 1: Test Questions - Why AI Needs to Be Governed

> Practice questions in the style of the AIGP certification exam (single-best-answer multiple choice, 4 options). Try to answer all questions before checking the answer key at the bottom.

## Questions

1. Which OECD AI Principle relates to ensuring AI systems are robust, secure, and operate safely throughout their lifecycle?
   A. Transparency and explainability
   B. Robustness, security and safety
   C. Human-centered values and fairness
   D. Accountability

2. Which term describes an AI system's capacity to produce outputs that appear plausible but are factually incorrect or unsupported by its training data?
   A. Bias
   B. Hallucination
   C. Drift
   D. Overfitting

3. What distinguishes a "narrow AI" system from "artificial general intelligence (AGI)"?
   A. Narrow AI can only process text; AGI can process any modality
   B. Narrow AI is designed for a specific task; AGI would match or exceed human cognitive ability across many domains
   C. Narrow AI requires no training data
   D. AGI is a legal classification under the EU AI Act

4. In the context of AI governance, which of the following best defines "automation bias"?
   A. The tendency of an algorithm to favor majority-class training data
   B. The tendency of human users to over-rely on automated system outputs, even when contradicted by other information
   C. A systemic skew in sampling methodology
   D. A deliberate attempt to manipulate model weights

5. What is a "transformer" in the context of large language models?
   A. A hardware accelerator used only for inference
   B. A neural network architecture that uses self-attention mechanisms to process sequential data
   C. A technique for converting unstructured data into structured data
   D. A data governance framework for AI training

6. Which of the following is a defining characteristic of AI that most distinguishes it from traditional software?
   A. AI systems always require cloud infrastructure
   B. AI systems can exhibit probabilistic, non-deterministic behavior that evolves with new data
   C. AI systems cannot be tested
   D. AI systems are exempt from existing laws

7. Under the OECD AI Principles, which principle emphasizes that AI actors should be accountable for the proper functioning of AI systems?
   A. Inclusive growth, sustainable development and well-being
   B. Accountability
   C. Transparency and explainability
   D. Human-centered values and fairness

8. What is "inference" in the context of a trained machine learning model?
   A. The process of labeling training data
   B. The process of using a trained model to generate predictions or outputs on new data
   C. The process of removing bias from a dataset
   D. A legal term for model certification

9. Which best describes a "token" in the context of large language models?
   A. A unit of text (e.g., a word or sub-word) that a model processes as input or output
   B. A cryptographic key used to access model APIs
   C. A unit of compute measured in GPU-hours
   D. A legal instrument granting IP rights to training data

10. Why do unique characteristics of AI (e.g., opacity, autonomy, and evolving behavior) create governance challenges not typically seen with traditional IT systems?
    A. They make AI systems incompatible with any regulation
    B. They can make it difficult to predict, explain, or assign accountability for system outputs
    C. They eliminate the need for human oversight
    D. They guarantee compliance with privacy laws

## Answers

1. B
2. B
3. B
4. B
5. B
6. B
7. B
8. B
9. A
10. B
