# Chapter 12: Test Questions - Preparing to Deploy AI

> Practice questions in the style of the AIGP certification exam (single-best-answer multiple choice, 4 options). Try to answer all questions before checking the answer key at the bottom.

## Questions

1. What does "TEVV" stand for in the context of preparing AI systems for deployment?
   A. Testing, Evaluation, Verification, and Validation
   B. Transparency, Ethics, Vendor Vetting
   C. Training, Encryption, Version control, Verification
   D. Technical Error Visibility and Validation

2. Why is an incident response plan important before deploying an AI system?
   A. Because AI systems never fail
   B. To ensure the organization can detect, respond to, and remediate AI-related incidents promptly
   C. Incident response is only relevant to cybersecurity, not AI
   D. It replaces the need for pre-deployment testing

3. What does "human oversight" commonly refer to in AI deployment readiness contexts (e.g., under the EU AI Act)?
   A. Removing all human involvement for efficiency
   B. Ensuring humans can monitor, intervene in, or override AI system decisions, especially in high-risk contexts
   C. Only applies to systems used by children
   D. A purely marketing commitment with no operational meaning

4. Which concern does "automation bias" pose specifically for human overseers of AI systems?
   A. Overseers may over-trust AI outputs and fail to critically evaluate them
   B. Overseers always under-trust AI outputs
   C. It has no relevance to human oversight design
   D. It only affects fully autonomous systems with no human in the loop

5. What is the purpose of a "model card" in supporting deployment transparency?
   A. A legal contract between vendor and customer
   B. A structured document summarizing a model's intended use, performance, limitations, and training data characteristics
   C. A payment processing record
   D. A hardware specification sheet only

6. What is a "pilot" deployment, and why is it used?
   A. A full-scale, permanent release with no monitoring
   B. A limited release to real users to observe real-world performance while containing potential harm before wider rollout
   C. A purely theoretical exercise with no real users
   D. A deployment exclusively for internal IT testing

7. What is the purpose of a "go/no-go" decision point in AI deployment readiness?
   A. To finalize marketing budgets
   B. To formally confirm, based on defined criteria, whether a system is ready to proceed to deployment
   C. To eliminate the need for further monitoring
   D. To replace legal sign-off requirements

8. Why is a "change management" process important for AI systems after initial deployment approval?
   A. AI systems never change once deployed
   B. Modifications (e.g., retraining, configuration changes) can alter the risk profile and require review, documentation, and approval
   C. Change management only applies to hardware changes
   D. It is solely an IT ticketing concern unrelated to governance

9. Which of the following best reflects why TEVV activities should occur before a go/no-go decision?
   A. TEVV results have no bearing on deployment approval
   B. They provide evidence of whether the system performs as intended and meets risk/quality thresholds
   C. TEVV is only relevant post-deployment
   D. TEVV replaces the need for human oversight planning

10. Why might regulators (e.g., under the EU AI Act) require documented human oversight measures specifically for high-risk AI systems?
    A. To reduce accountability for AI providers
    B. To help ensure humans retain the ability to identify and correct errors or automation bias before harm occurs
    C. Because high-risk systems are otherwise unregulated
    D. Because human oversight is irrelevant to risk mitigation

## Answers

1. A
2. B
3. B
4. A
5. B
6. B
7. B
8. B
9. B
10. B
