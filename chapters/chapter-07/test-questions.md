# Chapter 7: Test Questions - The Emergence of AI-Specific Legislation

> Practice questions in the style of the AIGP certification exam (single-best-answer multiple choice, 4 options). Try to answer all questions before checking the answer key at the bottom.

## Questions

1. Under the EU AI Act, which risk tier includes practices such as social scoring by public authorities and is generally prohibited?
   A. Minimal risk
   B. Limited risk
   C. High risk
   D. Unacceptable risk

2. What transparency obligation does the EU AI Act impose on providers of certain AI systems interacting directly with natural persons (e.g., chatbots)?
   A. No disclosure is required
   B. Users must generally be informed they are interacting with an AI system, unless obvious from context
   C. Disclosure is only required for high-risk systems
   D. Disclosure is only required if requested by a court

3. South Korea's "AI Basic Act" is best described as:
   A. A sector-specific law limited to financial services
   B. A framework act establishing foundational obligations and institutions for AI development and trust
   C. A voluntary code of conduct with no legal effect
   D. An amendment to South Korea's privacy law only

4. In the absence of a single comprehensive U.S. federal AI law, which of the following best describes the primary source of binding AI-specific rules in the U.S.?
   A. A single federal AI Act similar to the EU's
   B. A patchwork of state-level legislation (e.g., Colorado, Utah, California) and federal executive actions/guidance
   C. No AI-specific rules exist at any level of U.S. government
   D. International treaties exclusively

5. Under the EU AI Act, what is a "general-purpose AI model" with "systemic risk" generally associated with?
   A. Any AI system regardless of capability
   B. Models meeting defined capability/compute thresholds that could pose large-scale risks requiring additional obligations
   C. Only models used exclusively by small businesses
   D. Models that are not publicly available

6. Which obligation applies to providers of high-risk AI systems under the EU AI Act regarding post-market activities?
   A. No post-market obligations exist
   B. Post-market monitoring, including reporting of serious incidents
   C. Only pre-market obligations apply; post-market is unregulated
   D. Only applies to systems sold outside the EU

7. Which U.S. state enacted a law specifically creating consumer protection obligations tied to "high-risk" AI systems, mirroring aspects of EU risk tiering?
   A. Colorado
   B. Texas
   C. Florida
   D. Ohio

8. What role do "codes of practice" (e.g., the EU's General-Purpose AI Code of Practice) typically serve under AI-specific legislation?
   A. They are criminal statutes
   B. They provide a voluntary mechanism to demonstrate compliance with certain binding obligations, often developed with stakeholder input
   C. They replace the legislation entirely
   D. They apply only to government users

9. Which best reflects the EU AI Act's treatment of AI systems used for biometric categorization or real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces by law enforcement?
   A. Completely unregulated
   B. Subject to prohibition or strict conditions/high-risk obligations, reflecting heightened fundamental rights concerns
   C. Automatically classified as minimal risk
   D. Entirely delegated to private company discretion

10. Why have individual U.S. states moved faster than the federal government on AI-specific legislation?
    A. Federal preemption prohibits any state action
    B. States often have more agile legislative processes and have responded to visible local AI harms (e.g., deepfakes, hiring tools) absent comprehensive federal action
    C. The U.S. Constitution requires states to legislate on AI
    D. States are legally required to mirror the EU AI Act

## Answers

1. D
2. B
3. B
4. B
5. B
6. B
7. A
8. B
9. B
10. B
