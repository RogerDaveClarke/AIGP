# Chapter 14: Test Questions - AI Assessments and Audits

> Practice questions in the style of the AIGP certification exam (single-best-answer multiple choice, 4 options). Try to answer all questions before checking the answer key at the bottom.

## Questions

1. What is the key distinction between an "assessment" and an "audit" in the AI governance context?
   A. They are legally identical terms with no difference
   B. An assessment is typically a self-evaluation process, while an audit is a more independent, often evidence-based examination against defined criteria
   C. Audits are always informal and assessments are always binding
   D. Assessments can only be performed by regulators

2. What primary benefit does a third-party AI audit offer over an internal self-assessment?
   A. Guaranteed favorable results
   B. Greater independence and credibility due to reduced conflicts of interest
   C. Lower cost in all cases
   D. No benefit; they are functionally identical

3. Which international standard provides a framework often referenced for assurance engagements, including non-financial ones like AI audits?
   A. ISAE 3000 (Revised)
   B. GDPR Article 35
   C. NIST AI RMF
   D. OECD AI Principles

4. Why should an organization generally avoid using the same firm for both AI audit readiness support and the subsequent independent audit?
   A. It is cheaper to use the same firm
   B. Using the same firm risks impaired independence or the appearance of self-review
   C. Regulations require using the same firm
   D. There is no concern with this practice

5. What does "conformity assessment" typically refer to under frameworks like the EU AI Act?
   A. A process for verifying that a high-risk AI system meets specified regulatory requirements before being placed on the market
   B. A marketing certification with no compliance function
   C. A one-time check with no documentation required
   D. A process exclusive to low-risk systems

6. Which of the following triggers increased audit scrutiny, according to common audit practice?
   A. An organization promptly and transparently provides requested evidence
   B. An organization is slow to respond or reluctant to provide documentation, suggesting potential control weaknesses
   C. An organization has no AI systems in scope
   D. An organization uses only open-source models

7. What is a primary purpose of New York City's Local Law 144 regarding automated employment decision tools?
   A. To ban all AI use in hiring
   B. To require bias audits and related disclosures for certain automated employment decision tools
   C. To regulate only non-employment AI use cases
   D. To replace federal anti-discrimination law entirely

8. What role does the Sarbanes-Oxley Act's internal controls framework play as an analogy in AI audit discussions?
   A. It has no relevance to AI auditing
   B. It illustrates how formalized internal control assessment and independent audit practices can be adapted to new risk domains like AI
   C. It mandates AI-specific audits directly
   D. It only applies to privately held companies

9. What is a common challenge in conducting AI audits compared to traditional financial audits?
   A. AI audit criteria and methodologies are as mature and standardized as financial audit practices
   B. AI audit practices, metrics, and standards are still maturing, creating challenges in consistency and comparability
   C. There is no challenge; AI audits are simpler
   D. AI systems cannot be audited under any circumstance

10. Why might an organization pursue voluntary third-party AI certification/audit (e.g., against ISO/IEC 42001) even without a legal mandate?
    A. To demonstrate credible assurance of responsible AI practices to customers, regulators, and partners
    B. Because it has no business value
    C. Because it eliminates all residual AI risk
    D. Because it is required for internal-only tools

## Answers

1. B
2. B
3. A
4. B
5. A
6. B
7. B
8. B
9. B
10. A
