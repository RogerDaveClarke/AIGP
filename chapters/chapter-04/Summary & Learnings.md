## Summary
AI governance is a team activity, and its success depends on leaders sharing a common understand of their roles, willingness to cooperate and trust in the process. AI cuts across traditional domains owned by individual executives with the risk that it's perceived as an encroachment with common challenges being legacy structures, competing incentives and organization change which requires a leader to anticipate friction and design for it. One of the most common challenges is resourcing the effort with responsibilities landing on individuals with competing priorities and workload. Whilst there are solutions to these challenges, establishing Oversight is the starting point. Oversight is defined as the allocation and exercise of rights to ensure a strategy is executed with accountability, transforming principles into actions and addressing unavoidable tensions and tradeoffs. Within organizations it may span multiple levels which can be layered to enhance effectiveness. The chosen approach needs to be clearly defined and structurally embedded. The common operators are: 
- Board of Directors
- Chief AI Officer
- AI Governance Committee

### Roles and Responsibilities
**Board of directors**
 - Duty of Care: the board is sufficiently engaged and informed to understand the organization's AI strategy and associated risks requiring an ongoing understanding of how AI is being development, deployment and governed. The board should be able to confirm that risks are being responsibly identified and managed.
 - Duty of loyalty: assess that AI delivers longterm enterprise value and is consistent with organizational principles, stakeholder commitments and legal obligations.

Specifically the board needs to consider the following areas:
- Composition, structure and education: Ensure the board has sufficient expertise to oversee AI strategy and governance including assigning a committee and establishing an education program covering AI technological developments, emerging risks and regulatory changes. 
- AI Strategy and value creation: Evaluate how AI initiatives align with long-term strategry, competitive position and capital allocation priorities.
- AI Risk management and compliance: Ensure risks are integrated into the risk management framework, reliance on 3rd party systems, a management plan to comply with regulations and laws and a mechanism to evaluate and make public disclosures including material risks.
- Operational oversight: Determine how AI systems are currently in use, ensure the organisation has visibility into systems and provide monitoring and verify escalation pathways so that risks and incidents are made available to management and the board in a timely manner.
- Workforce strategy and talent acquisitions: Evaluate the impact of AI on the workforce including talent strategy, skilling and culture.

**Chief AI Officer**
The role sits at the intersection of business strategy, technology, people and organizational change and, is essential to an organisations AI strategy. The responsibilities include five common themes starting with the enterprise AI Strategy where, they design the roadmap, identify use cases and align investments with organizational objectives. Second, they need to establish a governance framework, guide and oversee how AI is responsibly developed, deployed and used. Third, is to implementation where they translate concepts into production projects integrating AI systems into business operations. Fourth is to prompt consistency and continuous improvement by promoting enterprise alignment both horizontally and vertically. Finally, establish AI expertise through training, awareness and other initiatives. 

The officer must have mastered key skills including strategic judgement they can assess potential use cases and focus attention on those most likely to deliver value, technical literacy where they must understand the capabilities and limitations of AI technologies to make decisions and guide cross-functional teams, building consensus which is essential to align a large and diverse group of stakeholders and motivate them to execute collaboratively and finally ethical leadership where their role is to set the standard in driving top-down commitment across the organization. 

In order for the role to be effective, the organization must clarity the reporting structure where, who they report to sends a clear signal about how serious the organization is and define their authority and decision rights. For the CAIO to be effective from the outset of their tenure, they need to build C-Suite partnerships creating a consistent cadence with business, technology, legal and other leaders to align on priorities, seize opportunties by centralizing visibility into AI initiatives, identify and prioritize high-value opportunities, track value by defining success metrics, monitor performance and allocate resources, establish AI governance as a foundation to drive better and more scalable outcomes in the short and long term, and finally, own change management by embedding AI capabilities into real-world workflows, orchestrate training, communications and incentives to drive adoption.

**AI Governance Committee**
A committee distributes responsibility across a leadership making AI strategy and governance more coordinated. This approach can work well for organizations starting out with AI and, to be successful, they need to 1) Define the committees mandate including intake approval, risk classification, build-verus-buy decisions, third-party model review and vendor diligence, pre-deployment approval, human oversight requirements, recording keeping, performance monitoring, incident management and decommissions, 2) Establish decision ownership boundaries, where it provides input and what must be escalated to the board, 3) Documentation responsibilities and decision rights in charter and 4) monitor legal, regulations, technology and industry decisions to inform all decisions 5) Select members who expertise matches the documented responsibilities (e.g. legal, privacy, IT security risk, compliance and HR), 6) Establish a consistent meeting cadence supporting timely decision making and a mechanism to address time-sensitive issues 7) Document all decisions and their rationale promoting transparency and 8) Provide timely updates to affected stakeholders to promote transparency. 

Internal Committees are operational and drive execution, external act as consultants. 

**Management Clarity**
Regardless of AI oversight being driven by CAIO or governance committee, the foremost question will be this: who responsible. The concerns can be addressed by clearly defining 1) Decision rights 2) Escalation paths and 4) Joint accountability for outcomes.
