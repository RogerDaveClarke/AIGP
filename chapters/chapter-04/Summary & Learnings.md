## Summary
Oversight is defined as the allocation and exercise of rights to ensure a strategy is executed with accountability, transforming principles into actions and addressing unavoidable tensions and tradeoffs. Within organizations it may span multiple levels which can be layered to enhance effectiveness. The chosen approach needs to be clearly defined and structurally embedded. The common operators are: 
- Board of Directors
- Chief AI Officer
- AI Governance Committee

### Roles and Responsibilities
**Board of directors**
 - Duty of Care: the board is sufficiently engaged and informed to understand the organization's AI strategy and associated risks requiring an ongoing understanding of how AI is being development, deployment and governed. The board should be able to confirm that risks are being responsibly identified and managed.
 - Duty of loyalty: assess that AI delivers longterm enterprise value and is consistent with organizational principles, stakeholder commitments and legal obligations.

Specifically the board needs to consider the following areas:
- Composition, structure and education: Ensure the board has sufficient expertise to oversee AI strategy and governance including assigning a committee and establishing an education program covering AI technological developments, emerging risks and regulatory changes. 
- AI Strategy and value creation: Evaluate how AI initiatives align with long-term strategry, competitive position and capital allocation priorities.
- AI Risk management and compliance: Ensure risks are integrated into the risk management framework, reliance on 3rd party systems, a management plan to comply with regulations and laws and a mechanism to evaluate and make public disclosures including material risks.
- Operational oversight: Determine how AI systems are currently in use, ensure the organisation has visibility into systems and provide monitoring and verify escalation pathways so that risks and incidents are made available to management and the board in a timely manner.
- Workforce strategy and talent acquisitions: Evaluate the impact of AI on the workforce including talent strategy, skilling and culture.

**Chief AI Officer**
The role sits at the intersection of business strategy, technology, people and organizational change and, is essential to an organisations AI strategy. The responsibilities include five common themes starting with the enterprise AI Strategy where, they design the roadmap, identify use cases and align investments with organizational objectives. Second, they need to establish a governance framework, guide and oversee how AI is responsibly developed, deployed and used. Third, is to implementation where they translate concepts into production projects integrating AI systems into business operations. Fourth is to prompt consistency and continuous improvement by promoting enterprise alignment both horizontally and vertically. Finally, establish AI expertise through training, awareness and other initiatives. 

The officer must have mastered key skills including strategic judgement they can assess potential use cases and focus attention on those most likely to deliver value, technical literacy where they must understand the capabilities and limitations of AI technologies to make decisions and guide cross-functional teams, building consensus which is essential to align a large and diverse group of stakeholders and motivate them to execute collaboratively and finally ethical leadership where their role is to set the standard in driving top-down commitment across the organization.
