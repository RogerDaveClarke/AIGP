# Chapter 4: Test Questions - Organizational Oversight of AI

> Practice questions in the style of the AIGP certification exam (single-best-answer multiple choice, 4 options). Try to answer all questions before checking the answer key at the bottom.

## Questions

1. Under general corporate law principles (e.g., Delaware), what duty most directly obligates a board of directors to oversee material risks, including AI risk?
   A. Duty of loyalty only
   B. Duty of care (including oversight duties)
   C. Duty of confidentiality
   D. Fiduciary duty does not extend to technology risk

2. What is a primary responsibility typically assigned to a Chief AI Officer (CAIO)?
   A. Managing payroll systems
   B. Setting organizational AI strategy and ensuring responsible, coordinated AI adoption and governance
   C. Serving exclusively as the organization's spokesperson
   D. Approving unrelated, non-AI vendor contracts

3. What is a common purpose of an AI governance committee within an organization?
   A. To replace the board of directors entirely
   B. To provide cross-functional review, escalation, and decision-making on AI risk and policy matters
   C. To solely focus on marketing AI products
   D. To eliminate the need for an AI inventory

4. Which of the following is a common challenge organizations face when establishing AI oversight structures?
   A. Too much regulatory clarity
   B. Balancing innovation speed with adequate risk review and avoiding governance bottlenecks
   C. Excessive board expertise in AI
   D. Lack of any AI adoption pressure

5. Why might a smaller organization choose an AI governance committee over creating a dedicated CAIO role?
   A. Committees are always legally required
   B. A committee can distribute responsibility across existing leaders without adding a new C-level position
   C. Committees have more unilateral authority than any single officer
   D. It is the only approach recognized by regulators

6. What is a RACI chart typically used for in the context of AI governance oversight?
   A. To calculate a model's accuracy metrics
   B. To clarify who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed for governance decisions and tasks
   C. To track AI training data lineage
   D. To score cybersecurity vulnerabilities

7. Which of the following best reflects why boards increasingly ask for AI risk reporting?
   A. Because AI poses no financial, legal, or reputational risk
   B. Because AI-related incidents can create material financial, legal, and reputational exposure requiring board awareness
   C. Because it is unrelated to enterprise risk management
   D. Because shareholders are indifferent to AI oversight

8. In organizations without a formal AI governance committee, where does AI oversight responsibility often default to initially?
   A. Nowhere; it is left ungoverned
   B. Existing risk, legal, compliance, or technology committees/functions
   C. External regulators automatically assume this role
   D. The organization's customers

9. What is a key benefit of involving the board of directors in AI governance oversight?
   A. It eliminates all legal liability
   B. It ensures strategic alignment, resource allocation, and accountability at the highest level of the organization
   C. It replaces the need for a CAIO or committee
   D. It is only relevant for AI vendors, not AI deployers

10. Which organizational challenge is frequently cited regarding AI governance committees spanning legal, technical, and business functions?
    A. Aligning differing priorities, vocabularies, and risk tolerances across functions
    B. A complete absence of relevant expertise industry-wide
    C. Legal prohibition on cross-functional committees
    D. Boards are barred from delegating oversight

## Answers

1. B
2. B
3. B
4. B
5. B
6. B
7. B
8. B
9. B
10. A
