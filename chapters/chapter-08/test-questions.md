# Chapter 8: Test Questions - AI Industry Standards and Guidance

> Practice questions in the style of the AIGP certification exam (single-best-answer multiple choice, 4 options). Try to answer all questions before checking the answer key at the bottom.

## Questions

1. What is a key distinction between a "standard" and "guidance" in AI governance?
   A. Standards are always legally binding; guidance never is
   B. Standards typically result from formal consensus-based development processes, while guidance may be issued unilaterally by an authority without the same formal process
   C. They are identical in all respects
   D. Guidance always supersedes standards

2. Which NIST publication provides a voluntary framework organizations can use to manage AI risks across the Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage functions?
   A. NIST AI RMF 1.0
   B. ISO/IEC 42001
   C. GDPR
   D. The EU AI Act

3. What is the primary purpose of national or regional "AI Safety Institutes" (e.g., US CAISI, UK AISI)?
   A. To issue binding criminal penalties for AI misuse
   B. To research, evaluate, and advise on AI safety and security, often testing frontier models
   C. To replace all existing regulators
   D. To manufacture AI hardware

4. Which organization is primarily responsible for developing ISO/IEC AI management and technical standards (e.g., ISO/IEC 42001)?
   A. The United Nations Security Council
   B. ISO and IEC, through their joint technical committee processes
   C. A single national government acting alone
   D. The World Trade Organization

5. What does a "crosswalk" document (such as those NIST provides) typically help organizations do?
   A. Translate model weights between frameworks
   B. Map and compare requirements/controls across different standards or frameworks
   C. Convert unstructured data to structured data
   D. Perform red-teaming exercises

6. Which best describes the EU's approach to involving standards bodies like CEN-CENELEC in AI governance?
   A. Standards bodies play no role under the EU AI Act
   B. Harmonized standards developed by CEN-CENELEC can provide a presumption of conformity with certain EU AI Act requirements
   C. CEN-CENELEC standards are mandatory criminal codes
   D. Only ISO standards are recognized in the EU

7. Why might an organization voluntarily adopt ISO/IEC 42001 (AI management system standard) even without a legal mandate?
   A. It has no practical benefit
   B. It provides a structured, auditable framework to demonstrate responsible AI management practices to stakeholders and customers
   C. It is only relevant to hardware manufacturers
   D. It replaces the need for any legal compliance review

8. What is a key benefit commonly cited for adhering to recognized AI standards and guidance?
   A. Guaranteed immunity from all regulatory enforcement
   B. Improved consistency, interoperability, and demonstrable due diligence across the AI lifecycle
   C. Elimination of the need for human oversight
   D. Avoidance of all AI-related costs

9. Which of the following best reflects the relationship between U.S. executive orders and AI standards development?
   A. Executive orders have no influence on federal AI standards priorities
   B. Executive orders can direct federal agencies (e.g., NIST) to prioritize certain AI standards, research, or risk-management activities
   C. Executive orders directly amend ISO standards
   D. Executive orders can only address non-AI technology

10. Why do organizations operating internationally often track both regional (e.g., EU) and international (e.g., ISO) AI standards?
    A. International standards automatically override all regional laws
    B. Alignment with both can support compliance across jurisdictions and facilitate mutual recognition or presumption of conformity
    C. Only one type of standard is ever legally relevant
    D. Regional standards are always identical to international ones

## Answers

1. B
2. A
3. B
4. B
5. B
6. B
7. B
8. B
9. B
10. B
